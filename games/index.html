My history with video games probably began around 1980, in Benidorm. I was four or five, sitting at a Pac-Man cabinet, when some foreign tourist dropped a 25-peseta coin — five duros — into the machine for me. I do not remember him. I remember that.

THE THREAD

From arcade reflexes to systems that eat whole evenings.

The games I remember most strongly tend to do one of two things: create a place I want to inhabit, or create a system I cannot stop prodding. Sometimes they do both.

That line runs from the Pac-Man cabinet in Benidorm through Doom, Civilization and Dune II, and into games such as Elden Ring. Technology changed; the compulsion to see what happens next did not.
